CSO Insight Report

Case study: Massachusetts EEA combined sewer overflow notifications (Archive of Massachusetts Environmental Data feed). Evaluation years 2022 to 2025 with 2025 held out; 2022 begins July 2. Descriptive rankings use fit-eligible notifications only and are not rainfall-normalized prioritization. All classes are counted as report rows, not distinct discharges. UNKNOWN IDs pool reports by permittee and are not established physical outfalls. The mean-volume baseline beats the rainfall model on pooled RMSE. RMSE support: 1625/1643 eligible paired reports and 1625/3075 eligible test reports.

Frozen analysis plan, amended retrospectively. The hash verifies content integrity and does not establish chronology. The first repository commit already included results; pre-result registration is unverified. 2024 is pooled with 2022-2023 for fitting; 2025 is held out. See plan status and recorded amendments.

Hold-out evaluation metrics (test year 2025)

Intervals are conditional event-bootstrap ranges. Spearman resamples train+validation events while holding 2025 counts fixed. RMSE bootstrap resamples paired test events without refitting models. They omit storm dependence, fitting uncertainty and measurement error.

The mean-volume baseline RMSE is lower than the rainfall model on the same paired events. This does not demonstrate generalization advantage for the rainfall model.

Rainfall threshold is positive at or above the eligible training+validation 75th percentile (at least three rainfall observations per identifier). Of 1643 eligible known-rainfall test report rows, 1635 have threshold support; 8 rows across 5 identifiers default negative because support is insufficient. These rows remain in the denominator; missing rainfall is excluded. This event-conditional fraction does not measure overflow detection or accuracy on non-event days.
